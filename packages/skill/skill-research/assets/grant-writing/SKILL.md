# Grant Writing

A proposal is a funded plan, not a literature essay. The reviewer must be able to state the problem, the gap, what you will do, why you can do it, and what changes if it works.

## 1. Read the call before writing

Record the funder, program, deadline, budget ceiling, project period, eligibility, page and font limits, required sections, review criteria, and submission system. A technically strong proposal that ignores the call fails review.

Write a compliance table:

| Requirement | Limit | Where addressed | Checked |
|---|---|---|---|
| Specific aims | 1 page | Aims | yes/no |
| Research strategy | 6 pages | Strategy | yes/no |
| Data management plan | 2 pages | DMP | yes/no |
| Budget justification | 1 page | Budget | yes/no |

If a requirement is ambiguous, ask the program officer or the user; do not guess.

## 2. One-page concept

Before the full draft, write a one-page concept:

- **Problem** - the concrete condition or gap, with evidence and consequences.
- **Current state** - what has been tried and why it is insufficient.
- **Hypothesis or objective** - the specific thing this project will establish or build.
- **Approach** - the work packages and the decisive experiment or analysis.
- **Feasibility** - preliminary data, team expertise, access, and resources.
- **Outcomes and impact** - what will be different if the project succeeds, and for whom.
- **Risks** - the main scientific or execution risk and the mitigation.

If the concept cannot be stated in one page, the project is not scoped yet.

## 3. Specific aims

Write two or three aims. Each aim is a coherent question or work package, not a task list.

```markdown
### Aim 1. [Title]
**Question:** ...
**Rationale:** [why this aim is needed and what is missing]
**Approach:** [design, data, methods, and the decisive comparison]
**Expected outcome:** [what will be measured or established]
**Interpretation:** [what a positive and a negative result would mean]
**Potential pitfall and alternative:** [the most likely failure and the fallback]
```

Aims may be interdependent, but each should yield a usable result even if a later aim fails. Do not promise a chain in which every step depends on an untested assumption.

## 4. Research strategy

Use this order unless the funder requires another:

1. **Significance** - the problem, its importance, and the evidence that current approaches are insufficient.
2. **Innovation** - the new concept, method, or synthesis, stated precisely. "Novel" is not an innovation claim.
3. **Approach** - the overall design, then each aim with methods, data, analysis, and validation. Include preliminary data and how it de-risks the plan.
4. **Feasibility** - team, environment, access, timeline, and contingency.
5. **Outcomes and dissemination** - what the project produces and how it will be used.
6. **Ethics, data management, and sharing** - as required by the funder and the field.

Make the logic visible: each aim answers a question raised by the previous paragraph. State the evidence for every claim about the state of the field. Cite preliminary work precisely.

## 5. Budget and timeline

Build the budget from the work plan, not the ceiling. Justify each category by the activity it supports: personnel effort, data collection, equipment, computing, publication, travel, and indirect costs. Check allowability and cost-share rules.

The timeline must match the aims and the budget. A three-year plan with one year of funded personnel is not feasible. Mark decision points and go/no-go criteria where the project could pivot.

## 6. Review criteria

Write a short reviewer simulation against the funder's published criteria, typically:

- significance and originality;
- approach and feasibility;
- investigator and environment;
- innovation;
- broader impacts or translation;
- budget and timeline alignment;
- compliance and clarity.

For each criterion, state the evidence in the proposal and the likely concern. Fix the concern in the artifact; do not add defensive prose without changing the plan.

## 7. Resubmission

For a resubmission, produce a response matrix rather than a new proposal from scratch:

| Reviewer concern | Response | Where changed | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|

Address the substance, not the tone. If you disagree, state the evidence and the change that reduces the risk. Do not claim a revision that is not visible in the text.

## Hard rules

- Do not fabricate preliminary data, collaborators, letters, or institutional commitments.
- Do not promise work the team, data, or timeline cannot support.
- Do not reuse a proposal for another funder without checking the call and the overlap rules.
- Do not hide a fatal weakness in a footnote; either fix the plan or declare the limitation.
- Keep the funder's required structure and page limits. A beautiful proposal outside the format is ineligible.
